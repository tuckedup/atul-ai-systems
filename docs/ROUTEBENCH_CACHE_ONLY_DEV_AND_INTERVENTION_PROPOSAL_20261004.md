## 2. Corrected explanation of the v3 / earlier-diagnostic discrepancy (CRLF)
My earlier statement that v3 "keeps one more demotion" than the earlier offline diagnostic was wrong in direction and mechanism. Verified from the stored responses: the single differing case is `gnd-71ce0e9a4754c173` (published label unsupported), whose candidate begins `(Source: CNN)\r\n` followed by a question. The model declared an uncovered `"\n"` at offsets 13:14, but those offsets slice the `"\r"` of the CRLF. The earlier diagnostic conservatively kept a declared span whose offsets and text disagreed; v3 resolves the text to the unique newline, finds nothing substantive, and turns uncertain into supported. So v3 releases ONE MORE demotion than the earlier diagnostic, which adds one false pass (the case was already a v18 false pass) and explains κ 0.5799 versus 0.5976. The rule was not changed to regain that label: it is a documented offset-resolution choice, and both numbers are post-hoc dev diagnoses.

## 3. Why the Reveal peak does not transfer, from cached v16 errors (same recipe, same strict rule)
| Population | n | supported share | sens | spec | balanced acc | FP / FN | median source chars | median claim words |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reveal (probe) | 124 | 0.51 | 0.889 | 0.951 | **0.920** | 3 / 7 | 451 | 8 |
| Original dev | 128 | 0.62 | 0.924 | 0.694 | 0.809 | 15 / 6 | 4,554 | 22 |
| WiCE (probe) | 126 | 0.51 | 0.531 | 0.919 | 0.725 | 5 / 30 | 5,498 | 23 |

1. **Different task difficulty, not a better recipe.** Reveal cases are 8-word single-fact claims about a ~450-character paragraph; dev cases are 22-word (CNN: 55-word, multi-sentence) summary sentences over 4,500-character transcripts or articles. v16 error rate rises with source length across all three populations (cached, pooled): 7.6% (<800 chars), 10% (800-3,000), 23% (3,000-5,000), 28% (5,000+). Length is confounded with population, so this is supporting, not proof.
2. **The error mode flips.** On Reveal v16 is over-strict (7 false rejections, 3 false passes): the 7 false rejections are claims needing mild world-knowledge or inference (ECB "mission" in Frankfurt, Porsche from Stuttgart, "IEEE member"), exactly the leniency the published annotators accept; the 3 false passes are single short claims about the right entity whose label is "unsupported" (e.g. "Luiz is a soccer player"). On dev v16/v18 are over-lenient: 14-15 false passes vs 5-6 false rejections, and the missed unsupported claims are the subtle ones (TofuEval Nuanced Meaning Shift 2 of 6, Reasoning Error 2 of 5, Tense/Modality 1 of 2, Mis-Referencing 1 of 1 passed by v16; Extrinsic Information, the blatant type, 2 of 20) plus CNN multi-sentence summaries (7 of 11 unsupported summaries passed by v16, 5 by v18).
3. **Prevalence and label source.** Reveal was sampled 64/class (51% supported); kappa at a different prevalence is not comparable to dev's 62%. Balanced accuracy is the fairer comparison (0.92 vs 0.81), and the gap there is far smaller than the kappa gap (0.84 vs 0.64) suggests.
4. **The 0.30 threshold did no work**: every Reveal score is 1.0 or <= 0.25, so 0.30 and 1.0 give identical predictions. WiCE (also long-document) reaches only κ 0.45 with the same recipe and a different selected threshold.
Conclusion: the peak is real but is a property of short, single-fact entailment with a lenient-label error mode; copying the recipe onto long transcript summaries reproduces neither the difficulty nor the error profile.

## 4. ONE proposed intervention (needs your approval; nothing run): a typed, asymmetric veto of v18 by a claim-relation audit
**What changes predictions.** v18 stays the primary judge. A case's verdict flips from pass to REJECT only when (a) v18 passes it AND (b) the claim-relation-v3 audit (Claude Opus 5.5, the already-frozen prompt and v3 aggregation) returns an explicit assertion-level "unsupported" whose issue type is one of six typed distinctions: `possible_vs_confirmed` (tense/modality), `entity_mismatch`, `unlinked_facts`, `purpose_vs_result`, `authorization_vs_payment`, `share_vs_increase`. Everything else - v18 rejections, audit abstentions ("uncertain"), audit "unsupported" of type `unsupported_addition`, `attribute_transfer`, `other`, and all audit "supported" - leaves v18 unchanged. It cannot create a rejection from an abstention and never un-rejects.
**Why it should reduce the remaining semantic errors.** Of v18's 19 dev errors, 14 are false passes, and the independent label audit (2026-09-29, written before claim-relation existed) found that the genuinely model-fixable misses are typed distinctions: tense ("coming segment" vs completed comparison), attachment ("police support the program" vs its cost), strengthening ("welcomed" vs allowed to stay). The other shared errors are upstream rater disagreement (5 CNN summaries), missing annotation context and transcript ambiguity, which no judge change can repair. A claim-relation audit catches those typed distinctions explicitly, and a veto limited to them avoids the audit's measured weakness: its other flag types fire mostly on supported claims (see below), so they are excluded rather than traded against false rejections.
**What the cache says (descriptive, in-sample, post-hoc):** applying the rule to the 128 cached dev responses gives κ **0.7134** (17 errors; 3 false passes fixed, 1 new false rejection) vs v18 0.6745. Honest limits: (i) the type set was fixed from the audit categories, but I had already seen the audit's dev outcomes, so this is not an unbiased estimate; (ii) the unrestricted audit veto gives only 0.6241 (4 fixed, 8 new) and adding `attribute_transfer` gives 0.6668, i.e. the benefit depends entirely on the type restriction, which rests on 12 flipped cases concentrated in a few documents (6 of the 8 wrong flips come from two MediaSum documents: the Haitian-migrant story and the Ivana/Melania Trump story); (iii) reaching 0.74 on dev needs a net of 4 false passes fixed, and this rule nets 2; (iv) all five CNN false passes and most other shared errors are outside its reach, so even a perfect typed veto cannot be expected to reach 0.74 on its own.
**What would count as evidence.** Because every TofuEval document available (dev, TRAIN, the 70-document census, the 40 and 30 evaluation samples) has been inspected, no unexposed labelled data remain except the sealed test. The rule therefore cannot be validated here. A valid test needs either newly permissioned, independently labelled documents (the data-quality pilot specification), or one run on the sealed test under your separate authorization. Until then it is a development hypothesis.
**Cost and mechanics if approved for a first, cheap step:** none on dev (all 128 audit responses are cached, evaluation is a computation). For genuinely new documents: one Claude-subscription audit call per v18-pass case (roughly 60% of cases) at about 29 s and 3.6k output tokens each, plus the corresponding v18 call (about $0.008 on the OpenAI key, currently without credit). Frozen decision rule: the six types above, no tuning, uncertain unchanged, full-coverage requirement, document-grouped paired bootstrap against v18, improvement criterion separate from the 0.74 target.
