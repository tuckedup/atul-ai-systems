## The frozen rule (opt-in, separately versioned)
v18 stays the judge. A v18 PASS becomes a REJECT iff ALL hold: the audit record exists, is `ok`, has the claim-relation-v3 variant id and judge-code hash; its case-level verdict is explicit "unsupported"; NO assertion is "uncertain"; and at least one assertion is explicitly unsupported, of a whitelisted type, with a VALID candidate span (offsets re-verified against the candidate actually graded). Whitelist (frozen, six): `possible_vs_confirmed`, `entity_mismatch`, `unlinked_facts`, `purpose_vs_result`, `authorization_vs_payment`, `share_vs_increase`. Excluded: `attribute_transfer`, `unsupported_addition`, `other`. Everything else is v18 verbatim: v18 rejections are never touched, uncertainty/abstention never creates a rejection, missing or failed audits leave v18 unchanged (and make an evaluation incomplete, not easier). The rule can never fix a v18 false rejection; its only gain is converting false passes, and its price is the supported claims it wrongly vetoes. The whitelist is NOT to be tuned on dev.

## Tests
Each of the six allowed types vetoes; the three excluded types do not; invalid, out-of-range, empty, non-integer, boolean and text-mismatching spans never veto; case-level and assertion-level uncertainty and unreviewed substantive text leave v18 unchanged; v18 rejections are preserved whatever the audit says; supported paraphrase pairs pass through while the nearby false claim is vetoed; missing/failed/foreign audit records leave v18 unchanged; both-direction accounting (false rejections fixed is always 0); freeze never overwrites. Mutation checks - removing the whitelist, the span check, the uncertainty guard, or baseline preservation - each fail 1-6 tests. Full suite 1121 passed, ruff clean.

## ONE bounded independent-validation proposal (not started; needs your approval, a vendor quote and separate authorization of any spend)
**Why not the sealed test or more dev work.** Every TofuEval document available has been inspected; reusing dev would only re-measure the fitted number. The sealed 263-case headline test (MeetB 143, MediaSum 80, CNN 40; 50 documents) is the only unexposed acceptance data: spending it on a rule whose ceiling is a few errors, when the readiness gate currently refuses it, would burn the project's only independent check. On v18's fresh-document profile (37 false rejections vs 5 false passes) fixing every false pass would only raise weighted κ from 0.52 to about 0.60, so this rule is unlikely to matter there. The test stays sealed.

**Design: flag-then-adjudicate on NEW permissioned documents, scored in both directions.**
- *Population:* roughly 600 candidate sentences (not 128) from at least 40 NEW documents with documented permission (the data-quality pilot rules; no TofuEval/AggreFact/RAGTruth/Reveal/WiCE sources; fingerprint overlap check against the protected index), natural summaries sentence by sentence, kept whole including awkward ones.
- *Machine stage (no labels visible):* v18 on all candidates (OpenAI key, ~$0.008/case, ~$5 for 600, requires credit) and, for v18 passes only, one claim-relation-v3 audit through the Claude subscription (~60% of cases, ~29 s and ~3.6k output tokens each, about 3 hours for ~360 audits, subscription allowance only). Freeze rule, whitelist and populations BEFORE any label is seen.
- *Human stage (two independent annotators + a third adjudicator, supported/unsupported/uncertain, per the pilot spec):* label (a) EVERY case the rule flags (expected about 3% of cases, ~18), (b) a uniform random sample of ~60 unflagged v18 passes, and (c) a uniform random sample of ~60 v18 rejections. Sampling probabilities are fixed in advance; estimates use inverse-probability weights. About 140 adjudicated cases, not 600.
- *Both directions are reported:* false passes fixed and new false rejections among flags (flip precision with a Wilson interval); the estimated rate of missed false passes among unflagged passes; the estimated false-rejection rate among v18 rejections (which the rule cannot fix, so the headline limit is visible); a weighted whole-population κ for v18 and v18+veto with a document-grouped bootstrap.
- *Success criterion (improvement, not the 0.74 target):* flip precision with a 95% Wilson lower bound above 0.5 - net errors are fixed iff precision exceeds 0.5. With about 18 flags that needs 14 correct (78%); with 40 flags, 27 correct (68%); with 60, 38 (63%). On dev the observed precision was 3/4 (interval 0.30-0.95), which cannot distinguish a useful rule from a harmful one. Anything weaker is reported as inconclusive; failure ends the rule, with no re-tuning of the whitelist.
- *Honest limits:* a gain of two errors per 128 cases (κ about +0.04) cannot be confirmed by whole-population κ at any affordable size, hence the precision-based criterion; precision is the right test of "does the veto do more good than harm". Human labels create a NEW reference, so the result is evidence about the rule, not about the original benchmark target. The 0.74 benchmark claim would still need the sealed test under its readiness gate.
- *Budget/approvals needed:* a capped vendor quote for about 140 doubly-annotated, adjudicated cases plus source rights for 40 documents (none contacted; the draft quote request exists in `docs/ROUTEBENCH_VENDOR_QUOTE_REQUEST_DRAFT.md` and would be amended to this sampling plan); about $5 of OpenAI credit for v18; subscription time for ~360 audits; no GPU. Stopping rule: stop at the first incomplete stage; no retries beyond the frozen one-retry rule; no held-out access.

## AMENDMENT 2026-10-04 (later): audit binding, power analysis, corrected validation proposal
No spending, vendor contact, model calls or held-out access. The whitelist and every original result are unchanged (`rule.superseded-unbound.json` and the unbound reproduction are preserved).

### Audit-to-case binding (`typed-veto-v1.1`)
`decide()` now takes the case (`case_id`, full source, full candidate) and REFUSES any audit that is not bound to it, leaving v18 unchanged and naming the failed check: expected case id; SHA-256 of the full source and of the full candidate, checked against both the stored hashes and the stored texts; the exact prompt (rebuilt from the case and hashed); the expected model identity (`claude-opus-5-5`); and that the stored structure re-derives from the stored raw response. Plus the existing variant/judge-code hash. `audit_coverage` reports v18 passes whose audit is missing, failed or unbound, so an evaluation cannot pass silently. Regression test: an audit of a DIFFERENT document is refused even when the case id and candidate text are identical (`source_hash`); an audit filed under another case id, a wrong model, a tampered prompt/hash/text, and a structure edited after the fact are each refused; on the cached dev data all 128 audits are bound to their own case and none to any other (rotated attachment fails all 128). The binding is a pure restriction: the post-hoc dev reproduction is identical (κ 0.7133825079030558; 3 false passes fixed, 1 new false rejection; flips unchanged; all 88 v18 passes have a bound audit). New frozen rule: `.local/typed-veto-v1/rule_v1_1.json` (hash `007e7aace6756354...`). Tests: 40 for the veto (binding mutations fail 5), full suite 1129 passed, ruff clean.

### Correction: statistical confirmation IS affordable - power analysis (`routebench/evalops/typed_veto_power.py`, `.local/typed-veto-v1/power_analysis.json`)
My earlier statement that a gain of this size "cannot be confirmed ... at any affordable size" was made without a power analysis and is withdrawn. Simulation (1,000 replications per cell, 300 document-bootstrap draws; all assumptions explicit and varied): N candidate sentences in documents of 10; v18 judges all; humans label EVERY flagged case (sampling probability 1), a random 15% of the other v18 passes and a random 15% of v18 rejections, with inverse-probability weights; documents are the resampling unit; per-document heterogeneity in prevalence and flag rates (flags cluster, as on dev).
Accuracy improvement = (truly-unsupported flags - truly-supported flags) / N; κ improvement = weighted κ(v18+veto) - weighted κ(v18); flip precision = truly-unsupported flags / flags (the veto improves accuracy iff precision > 0.5). Power = share of simulated studies whose document-bootstrap 95% lower bound is above 0 (precision: above 0.5).

| Scenario (flag rates on v18 false passes / true passes) | N (documents) | mean flags | mean human labels | power: precision (Wilson) | power: accuracy gain | power: κ gain |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 dev-observed (0.214 / 0.0135; precision 0.75; true accuracy gain +1.6 pts, κ +0.040) | 600 (60) | 18.8 | 106 | 0.60 | 0.57 | 0.72 |
| | 1,000 (100) | 31.7 | 177 | 0.81 | 0.78 | 0.89 |
| | **1,500 (150)** | 47.2 | 265 | **0.93** | **0.92** | **0.97** |
| | 2,500 (250) | 78.6 | 441 | 0.99 | 0.99 | 1.00 |
| S3 better veto (0.30 / 0.010; precision 0.85) | 600 (60) | 23.1 | 110 | 0.94 | 0.92 | 0.96 |
| S2 same flag rates, fresh-document profile (few false passes; precision 0.40) | any N up to 4,000 | 4.5-59 | 49-651 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.02-0.07 |
| S4 null (precision 0.5, no net accuracy change) | 1,000 / 4,000 | 47 / 189 | 190 / 762 | 0.03 | 0.02-0.03 | 0.08 / 0.17 |
| S5 harmful (precision 0.42) | any | - | - | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00-0.02 |

What this shows: (1) with N = 1,500 (150 documents) and about 265 human labels, a veto that performs as it did on dev would be confirmed with about 92-97% power; 1,000 would give about 78-89%; (2) a null or harmful veto is almost never declared useful by the accuracy and precision criteria (about 2-3% false positives, as designed); (3) **κ gain is not the same as accuracy gain**: in the null scenario the accuracy gain is exactly zero but the weighted κ gain is +0.008 and its false-positive rate climbs with N (7% at N = 300 to 17% at N = 4,000), because flipping passes to rejections shifts the marginals that κ corrects for. So κ alone must not be the success criterion; (4) under a fresh-document profile with the same flag rates the veto is net harmful (precision 0.40) regardless of N - the dev result may not transfer, and the study would reveal it. The real flag rates on new documents are the largest unknown; the assumptions are inputs, not findings.

### Amended validation proposal (replaces the sampling and success sections above; not started)
- *Population:* 1,500 candidate sentences from 150 NEW documents with documented permission (10 sentences each, kept whole, no exclusion of awkward ones), plus 20 calibration cases from 10 further documents; no TofuEval/AggreFact/RAGTruth/Reveal/WiCE sources; fingerprint overlap check against the protected index.
- *Machine stage, before any label is seen (rule, whitelist, populations and sampling probabilities frozen):* v18 on all 1,500 (about $12 on the OpenAI key, which has no credit) and one bound audit for each v18 pass (about 900 calls, roughly 7 hours of Claude-subscription time). No labels or predictions are shown to annotators.
- *Sampling, fixed in advance:* P(label) = 1 for every flagged case, 0.15 for every other v18 pass, 0.15 for every v18 rejection, drawn by a pre-registered seeded draw; each case keeps its weight 1/P. Expected about 265 labelled cases (47 flags, ~140 unflagged passes, ~75 rejections); the quote requests capacity for up to 320.
- *Human stage:* two independent annotators per case (supported/unsupported/uncertain), a third adjudicator on disagreements and uncertain responses, per the data-quality pilot specification. Uncertain adjudications stay in the denominators and are reported both as a separate class and under the two binary conventions (as supported / as unsupported).
- *Reported, each with a document-bootstrap 95% interval (documents are the unit; weights kept in every resample):* **accuracy** of v18 and of v18+veto and their difference; **weighted Cohen's κ** of both and their difference; **false passes and false rejections** for both systems (rates among v18 passes and among v18 rejections), false passes fixed and new false rejections among the flags, flip precision; the distribution of flags over documents.
- *Success = improvement, defined on accuracy AND κ together:* the lower bound of the accuracy difference > 0 AND the lower bound of the κ difference > 0 AND flip precision lower bound > 0.5. A κ-only improvement (possible with zero accuracy change) does NOT count. Failing any part ends the rule: no re-tuning of the whitelist, no second run on the same documents. This study measures the veto on new human labels; it is not a pass of the original 0.74 target, which would still require the sealed test under its readiness gate.
- *Costs outside the vendor quote:* v18 calls (~$12), subscription time for ~900 audits, no GPU. Vendor costs are requested itemized in `docs/ROUTEBENCH_VENDOR_QUOTE_REQUEST_DRAFT_V2.md` (drafted, NOT sent).
